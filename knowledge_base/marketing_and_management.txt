Marketing and Principles of Management (Class 12)

Marketing management involves planning, organising, directing and
controlling the activities that facilitate the exchange of goods and
services between producers and consumers, guided by the marketing mix -- the
"4 Ps": Product (design, features, quality, branding, packaging), Price
(the amount charged, influenced by cost, competition, and perceived value),
Place/Physical distribution (channels used to make the product available to
customers), and Promotion (advertising, personal selling, sales promotion,
and public relations used to inform and persuade customers).

A distribution channel is the path through which goods move from the
producer to the final consumer; it can be a direct channel (producer to
consumer, with no intermediaries) or an indirect channel involving one or
more intermediaries such as wholesalers and retailers, each performing
functions like sorting, storing, and providing credit that would otherwise
have to be performed by the producer or consumer.

E-business refers to conducting business activities using electronic means,
particularly the internet -- covering online buying and selling (e-commerce),
online banking, digital marketing, and internal use of technology for
communication, record-keeping, and coordination. Compared with traditional
business, e-business can reduce costs of physical infrastructure and widen
market reach, but it also raises distinct concerns such as data security,
digital payment fraud, and building customer trust without face-to-face
interaction.

Principles of Management, particularly Henri Fayol's fourteen principles,
provide broad guidelines for how a manager should perform their functions.
Frequently referenced principles include Division of Work (specialisation
improves efficiency), Unity of Command (each employee should receive
instructions from one superior only, to avoid confusion), Unity of Direction
(one plan, one head, for a group of activities having the same objective),
Scalar Chain (the formal line of authority from the highest to the lowest
rank, which may be shortened using a "gang plank" in urgent situations), and
Esprit de Corps (promoting team spirit, since union is strength).

The core functions of management taught at this level are Planning
(deciding in advance what to do and how to do it), Organising (identifying
and grouping activities, assigning them, and establishing authority
relationships), Staffing (recruiting, selecting, training and developing
people to fill organisational roles), Directing (supervising, motivating,
leading and communicating with subordinates to get work done), and
Controlling (setting standards, measuring actual performance, comparing it
with the standard, and taking corrective action where there is a deviation).
