Company Accounts (Class 12)

A company is an artificial legal person, created by law, having a separate
legal identity from its members (shareholders), perpetual succession, and
limited liability for its members (a shareholder's loss is generally limited
to the amount unpaid on the shares they hold).

Share capital represents the capital raised by a company by issuing shares.
Authorised capital is the maximum capital a company is permitted to raise, as
stated in its memorandum of association. Issued capital is the part of the
authorised capital actually offered to the public/subscribers. Subscribed
capital is the part of the issued capital that investors have actually agreed
to take up, and Called-up capital is the part of the subscribed capital that
the company has asked shareholders to pay so far. Shares can be issued at
par (at face value), at a premium (above face value, credited to a Securities
Premium account, which has restricted uses under law), or, in limited cases,
at a discount.

When shares are issued, a company usually collects money in instalments:
Application money (with the application form), Allotment money (once shares
are allotted), and one or more Call money instalments. If a shareholder fails
to pay a call when due, the shares may eventually be forfeited, meaning the
company cancels the shareholder's membership and can reissue those shares to
someone else; any amount forfeited is transferred to a Capital Reserve
(subject to conditions) if the shares are reissued at a profit or at a price
that, combined with the forfeited amount, is not less than the face value.

Debentures are a form of long-term debt/borrowing, not share capital -- a
debenture holder is a creditor of the company, not an owner, and is entitled
to a fixed rate of interest regardless of whether the company earns a profit.
Debentures may be secured (backed by a charge on company assets) or unsecured,
and may be redeemable (repayable after a fixed period) or, in principle,
irredeemable/perpetual.

Companies commonly prepare their financial statements in a "Schedule III"
(as per the Companies Act) format for the Balance Sheet and Statement of
Profit and Loss, which prescribes a specific classification of items (for
example, Shareholders' Funds, Non-Current Liabilities, Current Liabilities on
the equity-and-liabilities side, and Non-Current Assets, Current Assets on
the assets side), rather than the simpler format used for sole
proprietorships and partnerships.
