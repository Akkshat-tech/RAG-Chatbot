Business Studies Fundamentals (Class 11)

Business refers to any economic activity concerned with the production or
purchase, and sale, of goods and services with the object of earning profit,
carried on regularly and involving an element of risk. Business activities
are broadly classified into industry (activities connected with the
extraction, production or processing of goods, further split into primary,
secondary and tertiary industries) and commerce (activities that facilitate
the exchange of goods, comprising trade -- buying and selling -- and
auxiliaries to trade such as transport, banking, insurance, warehousing and
advertising, which remove hindrances in the process of exchange).

Forms of business organisation studied at this level include:
- Sole Proprietorship: owned, managed and controlled by a single individual,
  who bears unlimited liability and receives all the profit; easiest to form
  and dissolve, but limited by the owner's capital and managerial capacity.
- Partnership: two or more persons agree to share the profits of a business
  carried on by all or any of them acting for all; liability of partners is
  usually unlimited and joint, and there is generally a cap on the number of
  partners depending on the applicable rules.
- Joint Hindu Family Business: governed by Hindu Law, membership arises by
  birth into the family, managed by the Karta who has unlimited liability
  while other members (co-parceners) have liability limited to their share
  in the joint family property.
- Cooperative Society: a voluntary association of persons who join together
  to protect their economic interests, registered under cooperative
  societies law, run on the principle of one member, one vote, regardless of
  the number of shares held.
- Joint Stock Company: an artificial person created by law, having a separate
  legal identity from its members, perpetual succession, and (typically)
  limited liability for shareholders; can be a private company (restricts
  the right to transfer shares and limits the number of members) or a public
  company (shares are freely transferable, no upper limit on members, and can
  invite the public to subscribe to its shares).

Business services (a distinct topic from trading in goods) include Banking
(accepting deposits and lending money; also offering services such as
overdrafts, cash credit, discounting of bills, and remittance of funds),
Insurance (a contract in which the insurer, for a consideration called the
premium, agrees to compensate the insured for a specified loss -- based on
principles such as utmost good faith, insurable interest, and indemnity), and
communication and warehousing services that support trade.

Sources of business finance are commonly classified by period (long-term,
medium-term, short-term), by ownership (owners' funds vs borrowed funds), and
by source of generation (internal sources such as retained earnings, vs
external sources such as issuing shares/debentures, loans from banks and
financial institutions, public deposits, and trade credit).

Small business enterprises, in Indian policy, are commonly classified (for
example, under the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises framework) based on
investment in plant and machinery/equipment and annual turnover, and are
supported through measures such as easier institutional credit, subsidies,
and reserved procurement in some sectors, because they contribute
significantly to employment generation and balanced regional development.
